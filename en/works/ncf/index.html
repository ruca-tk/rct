Context

DG New Context Festival is a new annual event from Digital Garage — a cultural festival combining music, dance, drone show, and technology, held in the heart of Shibuya. This was the first edition, and potentially the first of many. The visual design was led by a separate designer. My role was motion and interaction: I came into the process early, collaborated on what was possible, and built everything that moves.

02

Direction

Because the visual design wasn't mine, my job was to understand the designer's intent and push it further through motion. We worked together across several online Figma sessions — not just implementing her decisions but contributing to them. The best interactions on this site came out of that back-and-forth, not from either of us working alone.

03

Key Decisions

01. Matrix Text The hero title doesn't appear — it arrives. On load, characters jumble in a matrix-style scramble before resolving into the actual text. The same effect fires on each section title as it scrolls into view: letters scramble, then return to their correct state. It reflects the festival's theme of collision and emergence — context clashing before something new forms.

02. Rethinking the Blur Effect The designer wanted blurred images for the artist section. I raised a consideration: artists and their management care deeply about how they're represented, and a blurred face as a default state could create friction with the talent. I proposed keeping the images sharp by default and using the blur as a hover reveal instead — moving from blurred to clear on interaction. She loved it. I added a bezier curve transition and a subtle scale effect to make the reveal feel considered rather than mechanical.

03. Logo Micro-Animation The NCF logo consists of three geometric shapes. On hover, each shape independently rotates to a random angle, scales to a slightly randomised size, and changes to a random colour from the site's palette. Every hover is different. It gives the logo personality without breaking the brand — playful and unpredictable, but always within the colour system. When a modal opens, the same logo icons appear scattered across the background in random colours, adding depth and energy to what could have been a plain overlay.

04. Mobile Nav Expansion The designer asked for a mobile navigation that grows in height when opened rather than overlaying the full screen. I built the nav to animate its height on open, revealing menu items progressively. It feels physical — like something unfolding — and keeps the experience consistent with the tactile quality of the rest of the site.

04

Design

The site is dark, high-energy, and dense — appropriate for a festival. The video hero sets the tone immediately. Each section — music, drone show, urban sports — has its own visual identity within the same system. The motion layer sits on top of all of it: the matrix text gives every section entry a moment of drama, the artist hover interactions add tactility, and the logo animations add a constant low-level playfulness that rewards attention.

05

Build

Built with Vite, Tailwind CSS, GSAP, Lenis, and vanilla JS. The matrix text effect uses character randomisation on a timer, cycling through random characters before locking to the correct value letter by letter. The logo hover randomisation uses Math.random() to generate rotation, scale, and colour values from a defined palette array on each mouseenter event. The modal background icons are positioned and coloured using the same randomisation pattern, spawned on modal open and cleared on close.

06

Outcome

The site launched for the February 2026 festival. The collaboration model — designer leading visuals, me leading motion — produced interactions that came out of the back-and-forth between us. The hover blur reversal in particular came directly from working through the problem together.

07

Reflection

Working from another designer's Figma file meant I had less context for some decisions than I would have had if I'd designed it myself. A few motion timings took longer to get right because I was working out the intended feel during the build rather than having it defined upfront. In future collaborations I'd push for motion to be specified — even loosely — in the design file itself. A rough timing note goes a long way.
